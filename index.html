Mantle Bridge.

A Mantle Bridge moves ETH, USDC and other tokens from Ethereum onto Mantle, a modular layer-2 that posts its transaction data to a separate data-availability layer instead of pushing all of it to Ethereum — the reason its gas stays cheap. You pick the amount, review the fee and the amount that will arrive before you sign, pay for the deposit in ETH, and your funds stay in your own wallet the entire time.

How the estimate is calculated

What a Mantle Bridge does

A Mantle Bridge locks or burns your asset on one chain and credits the matching amount on the other, so value moves between Ethereum and Mantle without any asset ever leaving self-custody. When you deposit, the bridge contract on Ethereum receives your ETH or token and Mantle mints the equivalent to your address a few minutes later. When you withdraw, the reverse happens: the asset is burned on Mantle and released on Ethereum once the network has finalized the exit. The amount that will arrive, the fee and the estimated time are all shown before you sign, so there is no guesswork at the moment of approval.

Why Mantle keeps fees low

Mantle is a modular rollup: it runs execution on its own layer but publishes transaction data to a dedicated data-availability layer rather than writing all of it as Ethereum calldata, a design independently tracked alongside other rollups. Posting data is the largest cost a rollup carries, so moving it off the most expensive chain is what lets a Mantle Bridge transfer settle for a fraction of mainnet gas, even when the network is busy.

What you can bring across

ETH and major stablecoins such as USDC and USDT are the common deposits, and the same route handles most ERC-20 tokens Mantle supports. The asset keeps its identity on the other side — bridged USDC spends as USDC — and can be sent back to Ethereum through the same canonical path whenever you want.

MNT: the gas token you need after a Mantle Bridge

Mantle does not use ETH for gas. The network pays transaction fees in MNT, so the first thing to plan for after a Mantle Bridge deposit is a small MNT balance to cover your first moves. Many people bridge a little MNT alongside their main asset; others arrive with ETH or a stablecoin and swap a small amount for MNT on a Mantle DEX before doing anything else. Either way, treat MNT the way the Mantle documentation recommends treating gas on any chain: keep enough for several transactions, not just one, so a swap or a withdrawal never fails for want of fee money.

Mantle Bridge fees and timing

A Mantle Bridge charges you the Ethereum gas to run the deposit transaction plus any small protocol fee; once your funds are on Mantle, activity there is paid in MNT and costs very little thanks to the modular data-availability design. Timing is not symmetric — deposits are fast, withdrawals are deliberately slower because the rollup waits before releasing funds on Ethereum. The table below sets out what to expect on each side.

Judge a route by the net amount

Compare any bridge route by the net amount received after gas and fees, not the headline rate. For small transfers the fixed Ethereum gas can dominate, so bridging a tiny amount is rarely worth it; batching a larger deposit spreads that cost across more value. You can confirm the amount that actually landed by checking your address on the Mantle network explorer.

Deposit and withdrawal flow, step by step

The flow is the same every time, which is what makes a Mantle Bridge predictable once you have done it once.
  • Connect your self-custody wallet and choose Ethereum as the source and Mantle as the destination.
  • Enter the amount; the route shows the fee and the amount that will arrive.
  • Approve the token if it is an ERC-20, then sign the deposit transaction and pay Ethereum gas.
  • Wait for Ethereum confirmations — your balance appears on Mantle a few minutes later.
  • To exit, start a withdrawal on Mantle, wait for the finalization window, then claim on Ethereum.

Why withdrawals take longer than deposits

Deposits only need Ethereum to confirm your transaction, which happens in minutes. Withdrawals wait for Mantle's rollup to finalize its state on Ethereum before funds are released, a security window that protects the network. This is normal for a rollup and is the reason the exit side is measured in hours rather than minutes.

Is a Mantle Bridge safe to use?

A canonical Mantle Bridge keeps your keys in your wallet and never takes custody, so the main risks are the ones you control at signing. Confirm the destination token's contract address, check the amount received against the amount you sent, and review the approval you are granting.

Approvals and allowances

Prefer a limited approval over an unlimited one so a contract can only ever move the amount you intend, and revoke old allowances you no longer use. The fewer standing approvals your wallet carries, the smaller the surface any single risk can touch.

The one rule that never changes

No bridge, wallet or support agent ever needs your seed phrase. Anyone asking for it is attempting fraud. A real Mantle Bridge only ever asks you to sign a transaction you can read in your wallet — it never asks you to type or paste your recovery words anywhere.

What the Mantle ecosystem offers once you bridge

Bridging is the on-ramp, not the destination. Once your assets are on Mantle they work across its DeFi — decentralized exchanges, lending markets, liquidity pools and MNT-denominated apps — at the low fees the modular design makes possible. Keep a small MNT balance for gas, use the native stablecoins and ETH you brought across for trading or yield, and when you want native ETH back on Ethereum, exit through the canonical withdrawal route rather than an unknown third-party path.

Mantle Bridge vs a third-party bridge

You can reach Mantle through the canonical Mantle Bridge or through a third-party liquidity bridge. The canonical route mints the official representation of your asset and is the reference path for large or long-term transfers. A third-party bridge can be faster for withdrawals because it fronts liquidity rather than waiting for finalization, but it adds its own fee and its own trust assumptions. For most users the rule is simple: use the canonical route described in Mantle's own documentation unless you specifically need a faster exit and have checked the third party's reputation and the net amount it quotes.
What makes Mantle a modular layer-2?
Mantle runs execution on its own layer but posts transaction data to a separate data-availability layer instead of writing all of it to Ethereum. Keeping that data off the most expensive chain is what lets a Mantle Bridge transfer settle cheaply even when the network is busy.
Do I need MNT after a Mantle Bridge?
Yes. Gas on Mantle is paid in MNT, not ETH, so keep a small MNT balance to cover your first transactions. You can bridge a little MNT with your main asset or swap for it on a Mantle DEX after you arrive.
Why is a Mantle withdrawal slower than a deposit?
Deposits credit on Mantle within minutes of your Ethereum transaction confirming. Withdrawals wait for the rollup to finalize its state on Ethereum before releasing funds, a security window that is measured in hours rather than minutes.
How much does a Mantle Bridge transfer cost?
You pay Ethereum gas plus any small protocol fee to deposit; activity on Mantle itself is paid in MNT and costs very little. Compare routes by the net amount received after gas and fees rather than the headline figure.
Do I need an account to use the Mantle Bridge?
No. You connect a self-custody wallet and bridge on-chain, with no sign-up and no custody. Your funds stay under your own keys the whole time.
Can I send my assets back to Ethereum?
Yes. The canonical route works both ways: start a withdrawal on Mantle, wait for the finalization window, then claim your funds on Ethereum. A third-party bridge can be faster but adds its own fee and trust assumptions.
What happens if my deposit fails?
A failed transaction reverts and you keep your assets; only the Ethereum gas spent on the attempt is lost. Nothing is credited on Mantle until the deposit confirms.
